## 1. How Galvanizing Services Are Priced

### Standard Pricing Models

Hot-dip galvanizing services globally are priced using one of three primary models:

**A. Per-Weight Basis (Most Common)**
- **Per kg** is the standard in Europe, Asia, Africa, and Latin America
- **Per pound or per ton (short ton = 2,000 lbs)** is standard in North America
- The weight charged is typically the **weight of the steel being galvanized**, not the finished (coated) weight
- Some galvanizers charge based on **actual weight** (weighed on arrival), others on **theoretical weight** (calculated from drawings)

**B. Per-Piece Basis**
- Used for standardized, repetitive items (fasteners, fittings, small hardware)
- Common for centrifuge-galvanized items (bolts, nuts, clips)
- Also used when items are too small for per-kg pricing to be practical

**C. Per-Lot / Minimum Charge Basis**
- Applied when loads are too small to justify a full kettle dip
- Most galvanizers have a minimum charge equivalent to 200-500 kg worth of galvanizing

### Key Factors Affecting Price

| Factor | Impact on Price |
|--------|----------------|
| **Steel thickness** | Thicker steel = heavier zinc coating = higher cost per kg |
| **Article complexity** | Complex shapes with pockets, overlapping surfaces, or difficult drainage increase handling cost |
| **Surface area-to-weight ratio** | Hollow sections and lightweight structural members cost more per kg because they pick up disproportionate zinc relative to their weight |
| **Volume/batch size** | Large recurring orders get 10-30% volume discounts |
| **Steel surface condition** | Heavily rusted or painted steel requires additional pickling = surcharges |
| **Length of article** | Articles approaching or exceeding kettle length require double-dipping = higher cost |
| **Urgency** | Rush orders (24-48 hour turnaround) attract 25-50% surcharges |
| **Loading density** | Items that cannot be racked efficiently reduce throughput and raise per-kg cost |

### Typical Surcharges

- **Small lot surcharge**: Loads under 500 kg typically incur a minimum charge ($150-$400 in the US)
- **Rush/priority surcharge**: 25-50% premium for 1-2 day turnaround vs. standard 5-7 days
- **Double-dipping surcharge**: For items longer than the kettle, 30-60% premium
- **Rework surcharge**: If steel must be stripped and re-galvanized due to poor preparation
- **Special chemistry surcharge**: Reactive steels (high silicon/phosphorus) that consume more zinc
- **Zinc surcharge/adjustment**: Many galvanizers apply a variable zinc surcharge tied to LME prices (see Section 3)

### Why Prices Differ Between Regions

1. **Zinc cost**: Zinc LME is global, but local premiums, import duties, and transport costs vary
2. **Energy costs**: Natural gas (for kettle heating) varies enormously - near-zero in Venezuela vs. $10-15/MMBtu in Europe
3. **Labor costs**: A galvanizing plant in India or Latin America has 70-80% lower labor costs than the US
4. **Environmental compliance**: European and US plants face significant emissions control costs
5. **Market competition**: Regions with many galvanizers (US, Germany) see more competitive pricing
6. **Currency effects**: Weak local currencies make dollar-denominated inputs (zinc) more expensive but make services cheaper in USD terms

### How Galvanizers Pass Through Zinc Price Changes

**A. Zinc Surcharge Mechanism (Most Common)**
- Galvanizers set a "base price" calculated at a reference zinc LME price (e.g., $2,500/ton)
- A variable zinc surcharge is added/subtracted monthly based on LME movement
- Typical formula: Surcharge = (Current LME - Base LME) x zinc pickup factor x weight
- Updated monthly or quarterly
- This is standard practice in the US, Europe, and increasingly in Latin America

**B. Index-Linked Pricing**
- Some contracts (especially large infrastructure projects) link the galvanizing price directly to LME zinc via a formula
- Example: Price/ton = Base service fee + (LME zinc price x 0.07)

**C. Periodic Price Adjustments**
- Smaller galvanizers simply adjust their price lists quarterly or semi-annually
- Less transparent but simpler to administer
- More common in emerging markets including Latin America

**D. Pass-Through in Venezuela Context**
- With the Ley de Precios Justos constraining margins to 30%, zinc cost pass-through is critical
- If zinc costs rise significantly, the regulated maximum price must rise proportionally, or margins collapse
- This makes accurate cost accounting essential under Venezuelan regulations

## 4. Zinc Absorption Rates by Product Type

### What Is Zinc Absorption Rate?

The "zinc absorption rate" (also called zinc pickup, zinc consumption ratio, or zinc coating weight) is the mass of zinc deposited as a percentage of the steel weight being galvanized. It is the single most important technical parameter for pricing galvanizing services.

### Typical Zinc Absorption Rates

| Product Type | Zinc Absorption (% of steel weight) | Typical Coating Thickness (um) |
|-------------|-------------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| Heavy structural steel (>6mm thick) | 4-6% | 85-100 um |
| Medium structural steel (3-6mm) | 5-8% | 70-85 um |
| Light structural steel (<3mm) | 7-12% | 55-70 um |
| Hollow sections / tubes | 6-10% | 70-85 um |
| Plate / flat bar | 4-6% | 85-100 um |
| Castings | 4-6% | 80-100 um |
| Small fasteners (centrifuge) | 3-5% | 45-55 um |
| Wire (continuous galvanizing) | 3-8% | 20-60 um |
| Pipe fittings | 6-10% | 70-85 um |
| Rebar | 5-8% | 60-80 um |

### Industry Standards

**ASTM A123/A123M** (US standard) specifies minimum coating weights:

| Steel Thickness | Min. Coating Weight (g/m2, both sides) | Min. Thickness (um, one side) |
|----------------|----------------------------------------|------------------------------|
| >= 6.0 mm | 610 g/m2 | 86 um |
| 3.0 - 6.0 mm | 505 g/m2 | 71 um |
| 1.5 - 3.0 mm | 395 g/m2 | 56 um |
| < 1.5 mm | 325 g/m2 | 46 um |

**ISO 1461** (European/international standard) has similar requirements.

### Factors Increasing Zinc Absorption

1. **Steel chemistry**: Silicon (Si) and phosphorus (P) in the "Sandelin range" (0.04-0.14% Si) cause thick, gray, rough coatings with high zinc consumption
2. **Surface roughness**: Blasted or heavily pickled surfaces absorb more zinc
3. **Immersion time**: Longer dips = thicker coatings (standard is 3-6 minutes)
4. **Bath temperature**: Higher temperatures (>455C) increase coating thickness
5. **Withdrawal speed**: Slower withdrawal = thicker coatings
6. **Steel geometry**: Thin sections and high surface-area items pick up proportionally more zinc

### PYGLARA Context

PYGLARA uses a **10% zinc absorption rate** assumption for pricing. This is on the higher end of the typical range (4-10%), which may reflect:
- A conservative/safe assumption for pricing purposes
- A product mix weighted toward lighter-gauge or complex items
- The specific steel qualities available in Venezuela
- A deliberate buffer to ensure profitability

For oil & gas structural steel (thick plate, heavy beams), a more typical absorption rate would be 5-7%.

## 7. Regulated Pricing Environments

### Venezuela's Ley de Precios Justos (Fair Prices Law)

**Background**
- Enacted: January 23, 2014 (Gaceta Oficial No. 40.340)
- Regulator: SUNDDE (Superintendencia Nacional para la Defensa de los Derechos Socio-Economicos)
- Originally established a **maximum profit margin of 30%** on costs for all goods and services
- Applies to all companies operating in Venezuela

**Key Provisions**
- Article 32: Maximum profit margin capped at 30% of the cost structure
- The cost structure must be registered with SUNDDE
- Prices must be "just" and cannot exceed costs + 30% margin
- SUNDDE has authority to audit cost structures and set maximum prices
- Penalties for violation include fines, temporary closure, and imprisonment of executives (up to 14 years)
- Companies must submit detailed cost structures to the regulator

**How It Affects Galvanizing**
1. The cost basis for the 30% calculation includes: raw materials (zinc, chemicals), energy, labor, depreciation, and administrative costs
2. If zinc costs rise, the maximum permitted price rises proportionally (since margin is calculated on costs)
3. The key challenge is that SUNDDE's cost verification process can be slow, creating lag between actual cost increases and permitted price increases
4. Accurate, detailed, and defensible cost accounting is critical

**Current Status (2026)**
- Following the January 2026 regime change referenced in PYGLARA documentation, the status and enforcement of the Ley de Precios Justos may be under review
- Historically, enforcement has been inconsistent, especially for B2B industrial services (vs. consumer goods which received more scrutiny)
- Many industrial companies have operated with margins above 30% in practice, particularly in B2B sectors where SUNDDE enforcement is lighter
- PYGLARA's documentation notes ~50% internal margin achieved, suggesting the 30% cap has not been strictly enforced on industrial galvanizing services

**PYGLARA Compliance Strategy**
- The 30% cap applies to the margin on **declared cost structure**
- There can be legitimate differences between actual costs and declared costs due to timing, accounting methods, and depreciation schedules
- Industrial/B2B services have historically faced less price scrutiny than consumer goods
- A new government may further relax or reform these controls

### Other Countries with Similar Price Controls

| Country | Mechanism | Notes |
|---------|-----------|-------|
| Argentina | "Precios Cuidados" program | Voluntary agreements with some mandatory elements for consumer goods; less impact on industrial B2B |
| Zimbabwe | Price control periods | Sporadic; mainly consumer goods |
| Iran | Government-set prices for key goods | Includes some industrial inputs |
| Egypt | Price controls on certain commodities | Mainly consumer staples |
| Cuba | State-set prices for most goods | Comprehensive state pricing |

**None of these countries have a universal 30% margin cap applied to industrial B2B services as aggressively as Venezuela's Ley de Precios Justos.**
